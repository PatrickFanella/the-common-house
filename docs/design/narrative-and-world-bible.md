# Narrative and World Bible

[Project home](README.md) · Proposed narrative framework. Nothing in the sample cast or mythology is approved canon.

## Dramatic foundation

The player wants to understand the people who have made room for them. Investigations expose facts that alter those relationships. Belonging develops through both giving and receiving care, and it can survive disagreement.

The recurring dramatic question is: what does trust ask of you after you discover something troubling about someone?

## Confirmed world direction and proposed details

The house maintains a useful radio network for isolated people and unofficial communities. Its work includes check-ins, messages, supplies, and reconnecting people. The setting is an alternate future after societal collapse. Proposed context: large communications systems have fragmented while locally maintained radios and relays persist. The collapse's causes, elapsed time, geography, and surviving institutions remain open. Give particular communication failures understandable causes; collapse does not imply that all technology or culture vanished.

Anomalous transmissions exist in the concept's proposed world. Residents have uneven knowledge, as confirmed by the user. Proposed differences include inherited operating procedures, direct experience, technical explanations, and uncertain beliefs. Document what each person knows and how they learned it; uneven knowledge does not mean everyone knows the same truth and merely withholds it.

## Player and tone

The player arrives as a newcomer and is a blank slate for self-insertion. Write choices that allow different attitudes without assigning an elaborate past. Belonging can arise through current acts and commitments. Personal revelations about the protagonist require further agreement; do not make them the hidden cause of the collapse or the house's mystery.

Favor suspense, mystery, and creepiness over fright. Unease can grow from an unexplained reply, a discrepancy in a familiar routine, or uncertainty about who heard a private exchange. Ordinary warmth remains credible.

Open source is a theme: shared repair knowledge, competing revisions to operating instructions, and disputes over who controls useful knowledge are proposed expressions. This does not prescribe the game's software license.

## The house

Proposed full-game spaces include a radio room, shared kitchen, workshop, private rooms, storage, and an antenna or roof area. Only the radio room, kitchen, and archive/workshop are needed for the first playable.

Each space should contain evidence of current use. A repaired kettle, competing labels on a drawer, or a makeshift privacy screen can communicate relationships before exposition. Private rooms require a meaningful basis for entry; ordinary locked doors need not hide case-critical secrets.

## Investigable memories

The player does not leave the outpost in present-day reality. Other locations can appear infrequently as flashbacks or memories that can be investigated. Their access mechanism and ontological status are unresolved.

For each scene, specify its source, perspective, omissions, what the player can inspect, and what can be corroborated afterward. The player's blank slate does not require memories to belong to them; resident accounts and recordings are possible sources. Keep a remembered claim separate from an observed present-day fact.

A proposed low-cost experiment revisits the same room in an earlier state. Additional environments should wait until this interaction proves useful.

## Cast functions

These are writing roles, not final character sheets. Avoid assigning a single personality trait or worldview to an entire identity group.

| Draft role | Everyday presence | Investigative contribution | Source of tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Station keeper | Covers shifts and remembers check-ins | Network history and operating habits | Treats some inherited rules as settled obligations |
| Repairer | Keeps shared equipment working | Identifies modifications and material evidence | Has altered equipment without explaining why |
| Long-term resident | Makes the house welcoming in small ways | Remembers people absent from current records | Wants control over contact with someone from their past |
| Newer resident | Shares the player's uncertainty | Notices changes others normalize | Fears that disagreement could cost them their home |
| Remote operator | Recurs through ordinary traffic | An outside account of local events | Depends on the outpost while questioning its decisions |

The sample case instantiates two residents and one caller using temporary names. It does not require all these roles as separate NPCs.

## Character sheet requirements

Before expanding a character, write: a present need, an ordinary pleasure, a contribution to the home, a source of support they need, a boundary, a consequential past action, what they know, what they believe incorrectly, what they will volunteer, and what would change their mind. A secret alone is insufficient characterization.

## Case and longer-arc structure

Each case needs a factual spine with a consistent timeline, at least two understandable perspectives, a decision made with adequate evidence, and an aftermath that acknowledges what the player did. Give mundane motives enough explanatory power that supernatural explanations are not automatically correct.

The longer arc can accumulate consequences across cases: a repaired relationship, an unanswered call, an altered operating practice, or a resident taking on responsibility. The final antagonistic force and ending are open. A singular mastermind is not required.

## Lore delivery

Deliver lore through something the player can use or question: a disputed instruction, a changed call sign, a recording with a known date, or a maintenance note contradicted by the equipment. Optional material can deepen context, but crucial decisions must not depend on reading every document.

Keep author knowledge distinct from character knowledge. If a phenomenon has rules that affect deduction, document those rules privately and honor them. Unexplained origins can coexist with observable regularities.

## Belonging, coercion, and privacy

Show how the household handles refusal, departure, dissent, shared resources, and obligations. Different residents may behave differently. Care should remain credible after a revelation. Some conflict can be repaired; some may require a boundary or separation.

Occult practice, pagan religion, coercive groups, and abusive behavior are distinct subjects. Choose specific influences and research them before using real traditions. Do not equate an unusual belief with harm. Historical Red Scare influences require their own research if adopted; the game is not yet set in that period.

## Dialogue voice

Let people discuss immediate work and needs. Keep procedural radio speech comprehensible and consistent without requiring specialist knowledge. Household conversations can be warm, impatient, funny, or evasive. Give characters reasons to omit information other than serving the mystery.

Avoid interrogations that reduce residents to clue dispensers. Include reciprocal questions and moments when the player receives help. Optional player-defined self-disclosure may be explored later; a fixed protagonist biography is outside the current direction.
