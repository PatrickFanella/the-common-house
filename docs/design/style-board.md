# Style Board v0.1

[Project home](README.md) · Proposed visual studies for owner review. Nothing here approves a
style, palette, prop, or piece of world canon. Names on notes and labels are placeholders.

Per [D-17](decisions-and-open-questions.md), every material, texture, and prop below is
generated by Editor code in `Assets/Editor/StyleBoard/`. No third-party asset file is used.
The studies build in their own scene; `OutpostBlockout.unity` is unchanged. They follow the
[art direction brief](art-audio-and-ux-direction.md): worn timber, painted metal, salvaged
furniture, handwritten revisions, cables that go somewhere, and repairs made more than once.

## Studies

### Radio desk

![Radio desk study](style-board/radio-desk.jpg)

An older receiver with a newer module strapped to it with copper, a replacement red knob, a
splinted desk leg, and a switch whose printed label has been corrected by hand. Covered food
and a mug stand in for care from a resident. Warm lamp light and cool equipment glow share
the scene.

### Radio desk, later

![Radio desk study at a later hour](style-board/radio-desk-later.jpg)

The same desk with one routine changed: the lamp is off, the food is gone, the mug is on its
side, and the chair has been pushed back. This tests the brief's question of whether an
altered routine can create unease without a visual horror effect.

### Kitchen table

![Kitchen table study](style-board/kitchen-table.jpg)

A pendant over a table with mismatched chairs, one repainted with a replacement seat. It
also has a re-wrapped kettle handle, a kitchen bell with a pull cord, jars with taped labels,
and a drawer label crossed out and rewritten. Cold light from the night window meets the
warm pendant.

### Archive and workshop

![Archive and workshop study](style-board/archive-workshop.jpg)

Shelves of tape boxes with handwritten spines, a reel-to-reel machine marked "slow speed
only", a shared tool board whose rule has an added clause, and a cork board with a roster
and relay sketch. A cool overhead tube lights the room, and a warm task lamp lights the
bench.

## Materials and palette

![Generated material swatches](style-board/material-swatches.jpg)

27 materials are generated at 512 px from fixed integer seeds: timbers, floorboards,
plaster, tiles, chipped paints and enamels over metal or timber, tarnished metals, paper,
card, tape, cloth, cork, and cable. Each has albedo, normal, and metallic/smoothness maps for
Unity's built-in Standard shader. A small `CommonHouse/LitText` shader keeps handwritten
labels lit by the scene, so they do not glow in dark rooms.

## Review questions

1. Is this level of material realism right, or should surfaces be flatter and more graphic
   so that documents and controls read faster?
2. Does the mix of older painted equipment, black polymer modules, and copper-strap repairs
   read as an alternate future after collapse, or as generic mid-century? The answer depends
   partly on Q-02 (what infrastructure survives and how long ago collapse happened).
3. Should corrections in a second ink stay a recurring motif for competing revisions and
   shared knowledge? If so, should residents have their own hands or colours?
4. Does "Radio desk, later" feel uneasy enough through absence alone?
5. Is warm household light against cool equipment light worth keeping as a tendency? The
   brief treats it as optional and not as a safety/danger code.

## Known limits

- Rendered with the project's current gamma colour space and no reflection probes, so metals
  look flatter than they would with linear lighting and probes. Changing colour space would
  affect the POC and needs its own decision.
- Props are built from boxes, cylinders, spheres, and tubes. There are no bevels, cloth
  simulation, or sculpted forms, and no characters.
- Label text uses Unity's built-in sans-serif font rather than handwriting. Small labels are
  hard to read at this resolution, which is itself relevant to question 1.
- Timber grain is stylised and repeats visibly on large surfaces.
- No audio is included.

## Reproduce

```sh
python3 scripts/capture_style_board.py
```

The command copies the project into a disposable directory, runs
`CommonHouse.Editor.StyleBoard.StyleStudies.Capture` in the pinned Editor with a graphics
device, and fails if any image or inventory file is missing. Output stays under
`~/.local/state/common-house-unity/style-board-*`. Inspect the images; a passing exit code
only confirms that they were produced. In the Editor, **Common House → Style Board → Build
Studies In New Scene** builds the same studies in an unsaved scene for close inspection.

The committed images come from source revision `5fa01ad` on Kvant
(AMD Radeon RX 7900 XT, OpenGL 4.6, Mesa 26.2.2). They are byte-identical to an earlier run of
the same generator code, from an uncommitted tree, on the same machine. Other GPUs or drivers
may differ.
