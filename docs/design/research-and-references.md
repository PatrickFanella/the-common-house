# Research and References

[Project home](README.md) · Research register dated 2026-09-24.

## Provenance and limits

The initial research was conducted during the design conversation. All three user-supplied videos were reviewed through available English automatic captions, not a complete audiovisual review. Automatic captions can mistranscribe names and terminology. The summaries below are paraphrases; verify precise quotations and timestamps before publication.

Developer descriptions and GDC session abstracts were consulted. A session abstract does not establish that its full talk was watched. The proposed game mechanics and story are original brainstorming, not claims drawn from these sources. This register preserves the earlier research; sources were not re-fetched during this documentation pass.

## Design research

- [Her Story — official description](https://www.herstorygame.com/about/): searchable interview footage as the central interaction. Study how one discovered phrase motivates another search.
- [Sam Barlow — Making Her Story, GDC session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023430/Making-Her-Story-Telling-a): session abstract centers the player's imagination. Full-talk review remains useful follow-up.
- [Orwell — Osmotic Studios](https://www.osmoticstudios.com/orwell-keeping-an-eye-on-you/): selecting information to pass to an institution has consequences. Our application concerns recipients and context within a community.
- [Chris Remo — Interactive Story Without Challenge Mechanics, GDC session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026087/Interactive-Story-Without-Challenge-Mechanics): describes Firewatch's responsive narrative goals without conventional player failure.
- [Ian Dallas — Weaving 13 Prototypes into 1 Game, GDC session](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025016/Weaving-13-Prototypes-into-1): relevant to the production implications of distinct vignettes. No cost estimate for this project follows from that talk.
- [Emily Short — Storylets: You Want Them](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/): content with prerequisites and resulting state changes. Candidate structure for conditional household scenes.
- [Welcome to the Game II — developer description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/720250/Welcome_to_the_Game_II/): digital investigation and movement through a vulnerable physical setting. Repetition, difficulty, and threat pacing require separate examination before adoption.

## Original influence inventory

User references: Still Wakes the Deep; Phasmophobia; Welcome to the Game 2; Dear Esther; The Beginner's Guide; Gone Home; Control; What Remains of Edith Finch; Disco Elysium; Life Is Strange; Her Story; Orwell; Firewatch; Law and Order.

User concepts: procedural; short stories; vignettes; noir; the Red Scare; deep lore; first person; cult; esoteric; dark magic; pagan; techno futurist; cyberpunk; DIY; open source; finding your family. The user subsequently clarified found family and belonging, and prioritized investigating people and uncovering unsettling truths.

These references express interests, not a feature checklist. The user subsequently selected case-based stories, an alternate future after societal collapse, and open source as a theme. They also selected uneven resident knowledge, a newcomer for self-insertion, suspense over fright, solo Unity development, and a POC before release planning. Present-day play stays at the outpost; other locations appear infrequently through investigable memories or flashbacks.

- [Hypnospace Outlaw](https://www.hypnospace.net/): a fictional network inhabited by distinct people whose lives the player affects.
- [Citizen Sleeper](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1578650/Citizen_Sleeper/): work, relationships, and survival under economic pressure.

These are suggested comparisons, not assigned prerequisites or claims of a full comparative study.

## Research still needed

The broad setting is now an alternate future after societal collapse. Narrow geography, time since collapse, and surviving infrastructure before choosing specific radio equipment, operating culture, architecture, or historical analogies. If using Red Scare history, begin with the [Senate historical account](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/censure/133Joseph_McCarthy.htm) and [hearing transcript collection](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/investigations/mccarthy-transcripts.htm), then broaden perspectives. Those sources are a starting point, not a complete history.

Research real cooperative living practices, community radio, the specific spiritual traditions under consideration, and accessible audio game design after narrowing the premise. Establish the desired player experience before importing real-world complexity.
