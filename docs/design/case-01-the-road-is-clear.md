# Case 01 - The Road Is Clear

[Project home](README.md) · Proposed test case. Contains author-only spoilers. Names, history, and supernatural events are placeholders, not final canon.

## Purpose and cast

Test an evidence-supported identity discovery and a consequential choice about contact. Give the player a reason to care about a resident before exposing a concealed connection.

- **Player:** a confirmed newcomer conceived for player self-insertion; learning radio shifts is the proposed opening.
- **Ren:** a resident who helps the player settle in. Previously used the name Mara.
- **Ivo:** the station keeper, concerned about repeating a past failure.
- **Ada:** a caller who once worked at the house and wants contact with Ren.

Use the radio room, kitchen, and archive/workshop. Ren and Ivo can appear in more than one room; complex schedules are unnecessary.

## Author truth

In the alternate future after societal collapse, the household relays messages between scattered communities. In this proposed case history, years earlier it relayed evacuation messages for people leaving a controlling organization. Mara was Ren's network name. Ada operated from this house and later moved to another outpost. Ivo once relayed Ren's location without permission, believing a caller was a trusted contact. Ren subsequently withdrew from the network and adopted a new name locally.

Ada's current call is genuine. She has a message from someone Ren once tried to help, but Ren wants to decide whether to receive it. Ivo removed an old log page because it documents his own unauthorized relay. He fears another mistake and is ashamed of the earlier one. He does not control Ada's present transmission.

The phrase “the road is clear” is an old agreed invitation to discuss contact; it is not proof that travel is safe or that every caller is trustworthy.

## Opening and inciting call

Ren leaves food by the radio desk and shows the player how to replay a missed message. Ivo explains the endpoint labels. Ada calls with the phrase and asks for Mara. The player can ask her name and request that she remain available for a later response. No countdown forces a decision.

At the kitchen table, Ivo suggests the call belongs elsewhere. Ren asks which channel it arrived on, then avoids further discussion. Their response establishes a lead, not proof of identity.

## Six core evidence records

| ID | Observable material | Supports | Access/recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | Current Ada call, recorded with transcript | Caller name and request | Automatically logged; replayable |
| E02 | Dated archive tape: Ada addressed by name, kitchen bell audible; transcript labels the sound | Ada's past presence in the house | Shelf label or Ivo's archive suggestion leads to it |
| E03 | Old roster: “Mara — workshop”; the same distinctive handwritten repair mark appears on Ren's current note | A possible link between Ren and Mara | Archive and opening note; close view renders legible text |
| E04 | Ren confirms the former name and describes the unwanted relay | Identity and the importance of consent | Ask privately using E01; does not require E03 |
| E05 | Removed log page retained among Ivo's maintenance papers, recording the unauthorized relay | Independent support for Ren's account and Ivo's involvement | Ivo can disclose it when asked, or point to it after discussing the gap |
| E06 | Ada recalls a mundane shared repair; Ren corroborates the detail when asked | Further support that the present caller is the known Ada | Follow-up call plus private conversation, available before final routing |

E03 suggests identity; E04 confirms it. E02 and E06 support the caller's connection but do not prove every present claim. E04 and E05 support the past breach. The missing log page is accessible through conversation; trespassing is not mandatory.

## Investigation flow

Allow the archive, Ren, and Ivo leads in different orders. Ren can disclose the central identity directly, while the archive supplies context and corroboration. If the player reaches Ren first, later clues deepen understanding instead of contradicting a forced reveal sequence.

The notebook records Ada's request, the unresolved identity, and attributed explanations. Optional hints point to replay, a private question, or an available archive comparison. Once E04 is known, Ren can choose whether to accept contact. Other evidence may help the player decide what to recommend but does not override Ren's agency.

## Routing interaction and resolutions

For this prototype, Ren initially declines immediate contact and authorizes a refusal message. The player may relay that refusal to complete R2 or keep investigating. After E06 corroborates the caller and the private route has been checked, a new private conversation lets the player offer those assurances. Ren then explicitly agrees to contact, enabling R1. Evidence acquisition alone never counts as consent. These are authored character responses, not a persuasion score.

R3 becomes available after E04, when the player knows the request concerns Ren and has heard their privacy concern. The interface identifies the household speaker and explicitly warns that Ren has not authorized that disclosure. Before E04, an attempted final relay remains a draft and points to the unresolved recipient. This gate preserves the intended informed decision in the small prototype. Revisit its presentation if it feels artificial.

A simple speaker/headset switch is mislabeled from an earlier repair. Comparing its diagram with the current connection reveals which position is private. The accessible routing menu states the actual recipients after inspection. No surprise interface trick determines the ending.

| Resolution | Prerequisite and action | Immediate result | Household aftermath |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1: Private contact | Ren explicitly agrees; player selects the private headset route | Ren and Ada speak; player receives only what Ren later shares | Ren invites the player to join a routine repair and acknowledges the respected boundary |
| R2: Refusal relayed | Ren declines; player relays the refusal | Ada accepts it and leaves a way to reconnect later | Ren remains available and appreciative; the case closes without the contents of Ada's message |
| R3: Public disclosure | Player knowingly sends Ada's request over the household speaker without Ren's permission | Ren ends the exchange; Ivo must confront the parallel with his past action | Ren asks for distance; a later conversation permits acknowledgment and a repair attempt |

Offer an explicit defer option while investigating. Deferring leaves the case open and all essential leads available; it is not a fourth completed resolution. Do not frame R2 as an incomplete or inferior ending. R3 need not be an attractive choice, but its recipient and consequences must be understandable.

## Optional investigative memory experiment

After the baseline case works, consider a brief reconstruction associated with E02: an earlier state of the radio room or kitchen that the player can inspect. It takes place through a memory or flashback while the player remains at the outpost in present-day reality. Keep its source and reliability explicit; the access mechanism is still open.

For this experiment, the scene may contextualize an existing clue, but it must not add a seventh required evidence record or replace the transcript-only route. The proposed six-record baseline remains completable without it. Do not invent a personal memory for the blank-slate protagonist.

## Unsettling final beat

After the ordinary case resolution, an unattended receiver plays a brief exchange containing a phrase the player used in a private household conversation. The scene establishes that the microphone was off. Its recording and captions remain available. No explanation is provided in this prototype.

This proposed anomaly opens a future question; it must not retroactively invalidate the evidence or Ada's identity. Remove or revise it if playtests show it overwhelms the human case.

## Test routes

Exercise archive-first and conversation-first discovery; replay every recording; defer and resume; complete R1, R2, and R3; save/reload immediately before and after sending; and complete a transcript-only route. Confirm each path reaches the appropriate aftermath once and preserves which evidence was shared with whom.
