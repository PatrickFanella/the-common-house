# Home-base graphic language

Proposed asset direction derived from the ten graphic references supplied by
the owner on 2026-09-29. D-38 in the
[decision register](decisions-and-open-questions.md) confirms DedSec as an
aesthetic and attitude influence. This study interprets the images for the
[telecommunications home base](home-base.md); individual designs, slogans,
and resident tastes remain provisional.

## What the references contribute

The images combine black-and-white comic drawing, halftone shading, photocopy
texture, cut paper, repeated motifs, and aggressive changes of scale. Some
build faces from text or distort a wireframe with displaced scanlines. Others
overlay old desktop windows and mouse pointers on comic panels. Acid lime and
violet cut through the monochrome base; cyan and pink appear in smaller patches.

The useful attitude is playful, resourceful, and irreverent: people making
their own media with equipment and visual scraps at hand. Original station
graphics and repair notices can carry that attitude without making every room
or resident look alike.

| Reference device | Proposed environment use |
|---|---|
| Halftone and photocopied linework | Printed notices, zines, workbench panels, and layered wall posters |
| Torn diagonal collage and offset printing | Replaced corporate notices and handmade station identifiers |
| Repeated small motifs | Sticker sheets, equipment labels, or a local print experiment |
| Desktop windows and clustered pointers | A poster language associated with salvaged computing equipment |
| ASCII silhouettes and wireframe glitches | Small graphic studies near terminals, distinct from essential readouts |
| Sparse fluorescent accents | One dominant accent per graphic, with monochrome surroundings |

These are proposed placements, not assigned belongings or new evidence.
Create motifs from the house's observable work: connectors, patch panels,
broadcast rings, tools, keys, and repair marks. Skull imagery in the references
does not establish a faction emblem for this household.

## Applying it to the office conversion

Keep the former telecommunications office readable beneath the adaptations:
corporate room numbers, equipment cabinets, service counters, cable routes,
and meeting-room furniture. Let resident-made graphics accumulate on selected
surfaces rather than covering every wall. Contrast that density with quiet
areas for sleeping, eating, and private conversation.

The existing procedural kit's clean stripes and broadcast-ring panels are a
first study. A later asset slice can add a few original collage prints,
different printing treatments, taped revisions, and signs of repeated repair.
Use large silhouettes, strong light-dark separation, and limited small text so
the designs remain readable through D-24's small textures and soft frame.

Atmospheric graphics must leave navigation, controls, recipient information,
and clues legible. Essential evidence still needs its transcript-equivalent
presentation. In-world decoration may reflect what residents know; private
author truth about the incident and surviving ASI does not become background
signage through this art study.

## Reference and asset boundary

The ten supplied images remain visual references in the owner's attachment
storage. Their source files and DedSec marks have not been imported into the
Unity asset set. Any implementation should use original artwork and record
its generation or approved source in the existing provenance manifest. This
document adds no final assets, character histories, case content, mechanics,
or queued implementation task.
