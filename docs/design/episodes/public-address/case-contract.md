## Author truth and chronology

Bex copied a queued audio segment into her news rundown without checking its channel label. The private caller, Nessa, had requested a conversation with Bex only. A delayed log and the current rundown show that the public playback used that copied segment. No supernatural breach is required. A retraction cannot recall copies already received.

The original events described in stored sources precede their arrival here. Present-day accounts follow local receipt and inspection. Original device labels, receipt order, author chronology, and character interpretation are separate. Stored sources replay identically. Contemporary replies are to contemporary people or the explicitly identified current endpoint; no participant in a stored recording is contacted through playback.

The exact reveal is: The leak was an ordinary editorial act. Bex can stop spreading the recording, identify her error, and offer a repair Nessa chooses; she cannot purchase forgiveness through a broadcast.

Remain unknown: How many people kept copies and whether every listener heard the correction. Nessa's reason for the original private call stays private and is unnecessary to solve the routing error.
