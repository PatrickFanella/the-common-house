# Episodic Story and Case Writing

[Project home](README.md) · Confirmed writing priorities with proposed authoring tools. Examples are story seeds, not new baseline cases.

## Emotional foundation

Stories center on a character dealing with something and releasing emotion. Grief, shame, resentment, longing, or an unresolved obligation can drive a case. Release can take the form of speaking, mourning, laughter, anger, a boundary, or a changed commitment. It need not mean reconciliation, forgiveness, or complete recovery.

The running theme is: you can leave a refuge, remain physically unharmed, and become unable to return. The same tension applies to a former home, relationship, role, or version of oneself. Let characters respond differently; avoid making every resolution deliver the same lesson about moving on.

## Five writing priorities

1. **Investigation as a social act.** Asking, consulting, sharing, and withholding affect relationships. Residents supply context and disagreement, receive help, and have boundaries. Technical expertise can create a reason to ask someone for help.
2. **The radio's unsettling possibilities.** Establish ordinary listening habits so a missing show, altered sign-off, unexpected reply, or personally familiar fragment can disturb them.
3. **Cases that leave a domestic trace.** Carry consequences into routines, rooms, responsibilities, and objects after the immediate radio response.
4. **A refuge with a difficult history.** People brought together by circumstance have their own histories; the building and household have histories to unravel too. Care can remain real after a troubling discovery.
5. **A network nobody fully understands.** Distinguish author truth from character explanations. A human case can close while its signal's origin remains partly unresolved.

## Episodic structure and global arc

Standalone stories connect to a global arc. The user supplied The X-Files, Fringe, and Law and Order as structural references. This is creative direction, not a season plan, episode count, or requirement that every case expose the same conspiracy.

Proposed sequence: ordinary household situation → disruptive transmission → social investigation → consequential act or refusal → emotional aftermath → a domestic trace. Cases can vary this order. Each needs a consistent factual spine and a human question that can reach a meaningful outcome.

Let larger discoveries accumulate through repeated anomalies, operating practices, infrastructure evidence, and changing relationships. A case does not have to reveal the AI to contribute. Do not attribute every human conflict or signal to its orchestration.

## Source-bound storytelling

The emotional connection determines why a signal matters. Its source determines what it can reveal. A camera has a frame; a microphone has a location and range; text has handles, pauses, omissions, and missing tone. An absent detail is not proof of what happened outside the source's coverage.

| Medium | Useful story material | Limits to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Phone call or voicemail | A request, interrupted exchange, or message left without an answer | No unrestricted visual scene; identity and date need support |
| Zoom call | Public performance beside offscreen private life | Camera frame, muted sound, disconnection, and participant perspective |
| AIM conversation | Handles, timestamps, abandoned messages, disputed tone | Missing context and uncertain authorship; no assumed access to private thoughts |
| Security camera | Motion alerts, partial arrivals, a doorway someone watches | Blind spots, clip boundaries, microphone coverage, live/stored distinction |
| Baby monitor | Intimate sounds whose context is unclear | Restricted coverage; a recognized voice does not establish everyone present |
| Dispatch recording | Urgency and a decision made with incomplete information | What the operator heard differs from what happened at the scene |

For each source, write its original circumstances, coverage, missing material, receiving/decoding method, possible alterations, and interaction limits. Keep an author chronology even when displayed timestamps disagree. Video and text arriving through radio need a fictional transport and suitable receiver; a plain speaker does not display a chat or video.

Essential reasoning remains available through transcripts, captions, visible metadata, and equivalent descriptions. A future visual feed is not approval to introduce an audio-only or visual-only clue into Case 01.

## Domestic traces

Proposed examples: a resident returns to communal meals; two people stop sharing a shift; a name joins the evening check-in; a photograph moves somewhere private; nobody sets a plate at an empty chair; someone broadcasts under their own name.

Specify who notices the change, when it appears, and whether it can evolve. A boundary respected or refusal relayed deserves a complete aftermath. Avoid converting every emotional consequence into a friendship score, chore, or reward object.

## Case worksheet

Before drafting, answer:

- Who is dealing with what, and what are they trying to recover, avoid, or express?
- What ordinary care or pleasure establishes their life beyond the mystery?
- What generated the signal, and what can it actually show or carry?
- What happened in author chronology, and what does each participant know or misunderstand?
- Which discoveries require consulting people, and what does sharing expose?
- What choices have distinct, perceivable consequences, including a valid refusal?
- What changes emotionally, and what remains unresolved?
- What concrete trace remains in the home?
- Does the case add a global-arc clue, and is that clue consistent with the private world rules?

## Proposed seed: the porch camera

A resident receives fragments from an old internet-connected porch camera. It shows the evening someone they loved was expected home. Motion alerts arrive out of order: an empty porch, the edge of a coat, a few words from outside the frame. The resident misses meals and shifts while waiting for evidence that the person arrived.

An unknown participant may be finding and forwarding more fragments in an attempt to help. Whether that participant is the AI, whether the feed is stored or live across time, what happened that evening, and the resident's history are all proposals requiring a factual spine before drafting.

One possible aftermath: the resident returns to the kitchen and asks to keep a radio there. They still want to listen sometimes, with company. This is an example of a trace, not a selected ending or new implementation task.
