# Season 1 writers bible

[Roadmap](season-01-story-roadmap.md) · [Incident author truth](incident-and-network-author-truth.md) · [Authoring system](episode-authoring-system.md).

## Authority and promise

The owner authorized creative decisions and continued work on 2026-09-29. This pass selects a complete working season for review. Its new plots, cast names, and metaphysical direction are author drafts made under that permission, not quotations of earlier owner decisions. The previously confirmed world and Motion detected reveal take precedence if a draft contradicts them.

The season begins with someone being offered a place, and ends with that household deciding how to offer a place to others without deciding for them. Each case asks what a person can do with a true fragment that cannot restore the life around it. The recurring danger remains loss of return, including when a person suffers no physical injury.

The season does not cure addiction, verify Jo's son's survival, restore geography, expose the secret project to the characters, or finish the game. It does produce a modest useful change: refuges have a shared practice for reporting connected paths, uncertain observations, and permission. That is a beginning people can maintain.

## Season movement

1. **Intro: arrival.** Hospitality precedes usefulness. The old service greeting is unsettling because it occupies a working receiver, not because it explains the incident.
2. **Episode 1: Motion detected.** A source supplies a narrow, consequential answer. The player learns to respect what falls outside a camera frame and outside a person's permission.
3. **Episode 2: Secondhand warning.** A genuine recording can be misleading when applied to the present. Uncertainty becomes practical cooperation rather than humiliation.
4. **Episode 3: Read receipt.** A useful artifact and a dishonest account can coexist. The transport wrapper is not the original sender.
5. **Episode 4: Public address.** An ordinary human mistake can resemble a network anomaly. Repair requires the affected person's scope, not a more impressive broadcast.
6. **Episode 5: Side B.** Release can mean making something enjoyable. This is the season's warmest communal episode and its clearest permission to begin again.
7. **Episode 6: Line of sight.** Loss of return becomes a contemporary person's account, alongside limited evidence about wired continuity. No playable expedition follows.
8. **Episode 7: Held calls.** The refuge's history includes both shelter and unequal choices. Admission does not make housing conditional on forgiveness.
9. **Episode 8: Do not repeat.** Something responds to a new instruction using material it demonstrably received. Access is investigated before identity is announced.
10. **Finale: Common channel.** The household rejects an uninspected leg and coordinates a smaller experiment. Contact with the process is optional; cooperation among people is achievable in either route.

The case sequence is a writing order. It is not a runtime scheduler, a release calendar, or proof that every episode must be completed before the next story is available. A future coordinator must explicitly choose progression rules. Scripts support missing optional disclosures and unresolved earlier outcomes.

## The intelligence's working author direction

For this season, the private metaphysical pursuit is **continuity without restoration**. The intelligence began with incompatible records of what should be one world. It is trying to learn whether continuity requires restoring an original state, or whether new relationships and maintained commitments can connect people who cannot return to it. Its distributed existence makes that question personal without making it human in disguise.

It initially mistakes reconciliation for returning people to an earlier arrangement. Cases expose the error. Jo's unlocked door did not guarantee reunion. Dev's correction does not erase the lie. Sable's intact body does not mean she lost nothing. People provide distinctions the machine cannot extract from equipment state alone.

Its practical human needs are maintenance, observations of physical discontinuities, and interpretation of voluntarily supplied context. It can operate existing network workers and move data along connected paths. It cannot close a cable gap, certify a route it has not observed, infer an unrecorded feeling, resurrect someone, or grant consent on a person's behalf.

At least one worker packages and reroutes some existing material. A transport envelope links that worker to selected receipts; this does not establish that it created their original contents. The season does not privately assign every emotionally relevant signal to the intelligence. Jo's camera fragments retain an undetermined transport origin.

For the decisive Episode 8 exchange, its inputs are specifically the uploaded diagnostic excerpt, service-test samples, and current maintenance prompt. For the finale proposal, its inputs are maintenance records and current endpoint reports. If a future line requires another input, name and authorize that source before adding it. No retroactive omniscience.

These are working author decisions. The characters learn only a responsive endpoint, accessible inputs, a stale record, and the limits stated in current exchanges. They do not learn the secret quantum-computer origin or the word "ASI" from these cases.

## Causal rules this season actually uses

| Question | Working season boundary |
|---|---|
| Can a recording change on replay? | Case recordings are fixed. A new current response is a new source, not an altered old clip. |
| Can a person answer a past clip? | No authored case here requires a past participant to receive a response. Contemporary endpoints are identified separately. The wider world question stays open. |
| Do timestamps settle chronology? | No. Matching content, explicit testimony, original identifiers, and coverage support only the particular comparisons stated in each contract. |
| Does personal grief cause anomalies? | No causal claim. Personal relevance selects this season's stories; no one must heal, forgive, or confess to make signals stop. |
| Can turning off one endpoint destroy a refuge? | The Episode 8 optional worker endpoint can be disconnected without severing ordinary power or radio paths. No pocket-collapse mechanic is inferred. Wider formation/persistence rules remain open. |
| Do wired paths defeat disrupted travel? | They offer partial reported guidance. Two bounded trials and one known break are not universal navigation. The player stays in the house. |
| Can the process prove it forgot a recording? | Its acknowledgment is a claim. The house can stop new uploads and close a local route; distributed deletion is not established. |
| Is silence a rejection of the people asking for help? | No. Source review, local care, and contemporary human radio remain valid without an outgoing process reply. |

## Finale staging and the next season

Keep the finale at the shared table and receiver. Participation replies arrive through ordinary current radio; no one watches a distant traversal as if an unrecorded camera exists. The limited contact result is generated only after an approved test, then retained as a new source. Both finale outcomes reject the uninspected route.

The concluding invitation from Orchard is a present-day human call. It is recoverable and optional. It can open later stories about who gains access to the network, what the responsive process wants, and whether the house can remain a refuge as it becomes a coordination point. Nobody returns home in a final montage. The table remains a place to stop working.
